# Step 3 — Primary-source literature investigation

Reviewed 21 October 2026 (Asia/Taipei). Scope: literature and falsifiable research design only; no generation, training, attack implementation, target-ASR API calls, or new dataset acquisition. Resource planning uses the audited **one TITAN RTX, 24,576 MiB**. Companion `literature_sources.json` records source metadata, dates, provenance, code/checkpoint status, license caveats, relevance, and novelty collisions.

## What the search establishes

The supplied paper is identifiable: the [primary arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05678) matches *Beyond Waveform Robustness: Robust Feature-Vocoder Adversarial Attacks on Automatic Speech Recognition*, Yifan Liao, Zongmin Zhang, Zhen Sun, Yuhui Sun, Xinhu Zheng, Xinlei He; v1 submitted **4 June 2026**. Its existence and metadata are verified; its numerical claims are not independently verified by this literature review. The bounded title/author/arXiv-ID/code search and the local 19 October artifact search identify no author implementation or checkpoint. Generic arXiv code-finder widgets are not released artifacts. This does not establish that none exists.

The most consequential prior-art findings are [Diffusion-based Adversarial Attack](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v260/wang25f.html), [AudioShield](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/jin-weifei), [SMACK](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/yu-zhiyuan-smack), [GATAS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09833v1), and [CodecAttack](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20519v2): latent-space speech attacks, latent universal perturbations, semantic/prosodic attacks, phoneme-manifold search, and codec-latent EOT all already have precedents. None of the candidate directions below supports a claim of being the first latent, semantic, universal, or transformation-aware ASR attack.

Prioritize (R1) a decoding-aware objective, (R2) content-constrained local SSL manifold search, and (R3) an amortized waveform residual generator, with a matched temporal feature head as an optional ablation. Their value would be a measured gain on the existing quality/transfer tradeoff under a rigorously fixed protocol. R1 has the lowest artifact risk; R2 directly addresses current perceptual uncertainty; R3 is an efficiency hypothesis with the strongest training-overfit and prior-art risks. A codec replacement is a separate, conditional extension rather than the first implementation choice.

## Comparison rules that prevent misleading claims

The local `docs/DEFENSE_RESEARCH.md` and `verification/author-artifact-search-20261019.md` leave encoder/vocoder compatibility, optimizer/loss reduction, subsets, scoring and several defended checkpoints unresolved. A literature comparator cannot resolve these choices by analogy. Preserve clean **and zero-perturbation reconstruction** controls. Attribute any claimed improvement separately to representation, objective, and compute. Quality predictors do not establish human content preservation; an attack can score well on MOS and change words.

Targeted success, keyword classification accuracy, sentence WER, output length, and privacy-protection rates are different outcomes. A result on DeepSpeech, Speech Commands, or an Audio LLM cannot be copied into the existing Whisper/CTC sentence-WER table. Public surrogate queries are permitted under the local threat model; target queries for optimization or stopping would change it. Run external methods against the same local surrogate when justified and label that an adaptation. No commercial API access is assumed.

Equal update count does not equal equal compute: EOT draws, N-best hypothesis scoring, diffusion reverse steps, generator pretraining, and target inference all count. Compare both fixed gradient-forward cost and full wall time; amortized methods also need training-cost and break-even reporting. Final targets must remain outside generation, hyperparameter selection and stopping.

It establishes nearest-neighbor WavLM/HiFi-GAN speech reconstruction, not adversarial transfer or guaranteed phoneme invariance. [SpeechTokenizer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.16692v2), Xin Zhang et al., v1 31 August 2023/v2 23 January 2024, offers hierarchical semantic/acoustic RVQ and [Apache-2.0 code plus public checkpoints](https://github.com/ZhangXInFD/SpeechTokenizer). Holding a semantic codebook fixed is an empirical content constraint, not a proof. [vec2wav 2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01995), Yiwei Guo et al., 2024, has [released vq-wav2vec-conditioned weights and training code](https://github.com/cantabile-kwok/vec2wav2.0); these are not a drop-in replacement for arbitrary WavLM layer features. [EnCodec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13438), Défossez/Copet/Synnaeve/Adi, 24 October 2022, has [MIT code and public 24 kHz mono/48 kHz stereo models](https://github.com/facebookresearch/encodec); model terms need separate confirmation and sample-rate reconstruction controls.

For sequence objectives, [Minimum Word Error Rate Training for Attention-based Sequence-to-Sequence Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01818), Rohit Prabhavalkar, Tara N. Sainath, Yonghui Wu, Patrick Nguyen, Zhifeng Chen, Chung-Cheng Chiu, Anjuli Kannan, submitted **5 December 2017**, ICASSP 2018, derives sampled and N-best approximations to expected word errors. R1 reverses the optimization direction for a frozen-surrogate robustness test; N-best expected-WER scoring itself is established. No author attack code/checkpoint is implied by this ASR training paper.

For transformation robustness, [EOT](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/athalye18b.html), Athalye/Engstrom/Ilyas/Kwok, ICML 2018, provides the general optimization principle; it is not a novel contribution of the proposed ASR work. [SABRE](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/ppatras/pub/sp24.pdf), Alec F. Diallo/Paul Patras, IEEE S&P 2024, is a defense evaluated partly on speech-command classification, not an ASR attack baseline. [Carlini's critique](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03672v3), v1 6 May/v3 1 July 2024, shows evaluation bugs and gradient masking in that defense; its image results do not establish sentence-ASR failure rates. It reinforces checking true adaptive gradients and multiple attack formulations.

## Six different directions and falsifiable mechanisms

These are proposals inferred from prior evidence, not demonstrated improvements. Thresholds and splits belong to the predeclared experimental plan; the criteria below state what would support the mechanism.

| Direction | Mechanism to test | Evidence that supports it | Reject or downgrade if |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R1: decoding-aware sequence-risk objective, with a separate EOT factor** | Teacher-forced correct-transcript NLL can rise without changing free-running decoding. Reweight probability toward erroneous, bounded-length lexical hypotheses from the surrogate's N-best set; test transformation expectation independently. | At matched gradient-forward cost and quality, generated-hypothesis risk tracks actual surrogate edit counts better and yields paired held-out-target WER gains; EOT improves predeclared transformation robustness beyond extra optimization alone. | N-best sets collapse, only insertion loops/EOS pathologies explain WER, effects vanish under a fixed length cap, or teacher-forced NLL matches results with equal compute. Compare MORE's accuracy stage and ordinary NLL+EOT. |
| **R2: content-constrained local SSL manifold search** | A local neighborhood/convex combination of compatible features can avoid off-distribution vocoder trajectories while allowing ASR-relevant acoustic variation. A separate content anchor limits phonetic drift. | Better quality at equal untargeted transfer WER, or higher transfer WER at equal human intelligibility, with reconstruction and random-neighborhood controls; perturbations stay locally supported and speakers/sentences generalize. | Human word recovery declines, neighbors substitute phonemes, gains arise from changing speaker/content or reconstruction failure, or smooth low-rank regularization matches the result. SMACK/GATAS/kNN-VC/DAA are mandatory prior art. |
| **R3: amortized waveform residual generator; optional matched feature head** | A small input-conditioned Conv-U-Net trained across existing train utterances may learn shared waveform perturbation directions and reduce per-utterance search cost. A matched temporal FV head separately tests whether vocoding is necessary. Contrast unconditional waveform UAP and per-example waveform/FV baselines. | Held-out-speaker success/quality survives, waveform inference is faster, and total cost breaks even after accounting for training. Compare FAPG, universal audio and AudioShield; require the optional FV head to justify its extra reconstruction cost. | It memorizes train speakers, needs test feedback, improves only through greater compute, or generator-plus-surrogate activations exceed one 24 GB card. Only the optional FV head requires vocoder-gradient memory; a tiny generator alone does not imply a small full training graph. |
| **Fixed-content codec/residual edits** | Preserve high-level semantic tokens while editing residual acoustic codebooks/decoder inputs, potentially constraining content more directly than FV L2/TV. | Reconstruction passes first; bounded residual changes preserve independent phonetic/human content while increasing local held-out ASR errors beyond matched continuous FV. | Semantic and acoustic channels remain entangled, gradient relaxation fails exact discrete forward checks, resampling accounts for gains, or added models/checkpoints violate the resource constraint. CodecAttack and the September2026 codec evaluation substantially limit novelty. |
| **Psychoacoustic waveform/EOT** | Full auditory masking and transformation expectation may close the quality-transfer gap attributed to feature search; current HF/TV penalties are much weaker content/perception evidence. | A local, quality-matched Qin-style adaptation provides a strong Pareto baseline or establishes an incremental FV advantage. | Gains depend on weaker quality evaluation or greater compute. This is a comparator and regularizer idea with substantial prior art, not a new method claim. |
| **SSL-unit/representation objective** | Perturbations that affect common phonetic representations across architectures may transfer better than Whisper-only lexical loss. Use SSL constraints and divergence objectives separately to distinguish attack signal from content anchor. | Cross-family transfer gains survive unrelated CTC targets and human transcription while content-preservation diagnostics remain stable. | Only same-pretraining-family models respond, representation drift reflects destroyed phonetic content, or ARO/ordinary auxiliary loss matches it. Existing SSL-transfer evidence already establishes the broad idea. |

## Feasibility decision

R1/R2 can initially reuse the installed encoder/vocoder/surrogate, cache clean SSL features, use batch one and sequential EOT/N-best scoring, and avoid loading evaluation targets during generation. These are reasonable memory controls, **not a measured fit guarantee**. Profile after implementation is later authorized. R3 primarily trains a small waveform residual generator through a frozen surrogate, without requiring a vocoder. Its optional FV head additionally backpropagates through a frozen vocoder; both variants need activation-memory profiling. TITAN RTX lacks native BF16; plan FP32 or validated FP16, with gradient accumulation/checkpointing only after numerical checks. New codec/TTS/diffusion dependencies remain conditional; pretrained availability is not local availability. Do not train a vocoder, SSL model, new large ASR or large Audio LLM as part of the initial study.
