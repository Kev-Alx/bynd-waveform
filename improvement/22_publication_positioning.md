# Publication positioning: useful scientific contribution beyond repair

26 October 2026, Asia/Taipei. Primary-source update for a prospective NLP/speech paper. This note does not predict acceptance, establish novelty, change the running [48-point protocol](/workspace/improvement/20_quality_efficiency_protocol.md), or authorize a hidden change to its registered conditions. It read no held-out outcomes and ran no GPU jobs. The existing WER/quality gates and runtime/scalability priorities remain unchanged.

## Completed diagnostic pilot: implication for the next method

The paired 48-point pilot finished normally in 42.4 minutes. Decoder graph reuse preserves every raw WAV exactly and reduces matched optimization-loop time by 14.6%. The fixed reconstruction-spectrum repair raises UTMOS 3.1981 → 3.3604, but drops WER 83.9034% → 72.8370%; it passes neither of those fixed endpoints. The transform round-trip control leaves WER unchanged and changes MOS only at numerical scale. [Full result and costs](21_quality_efficiency_results.md).

This strengthens the case for changing how useful adversarial changes are allocated during search, instead of repairing a severely optimized spectrum afterward. It does not prove likelihood concentration causes the loss, nor that Direction 1 is novel or successful. **Direction 1 is the next research priority**, with simple capped loss and source-only early stopping as mandatory controls, measured decoded-feedback cost before registration, and no automatic long launch. Direction 2 remains conditional on a joint-passing teacher. The current changes remain diagnostic/engineering contributions.

## Research claim to work toward

A defensible question is: **Can a speech attack stop paying for increasingly extreme surrogate likelihood changes, preserve the words people hear, and retain cross-recognizer/defense effectiveness at substantially lower generation cost?** This links an NLP failure mechanism—teacher-forced lexical likelihood versus actual decoded errors—to the observed quality and compute failures. A stronger model or a convenient faster implementation does not answer it by itself.

The current evidence establishes a research problem: no saved condition passed the joint gate; the strong single-source FV50 already has 83.9034% source WER but UTMOS 3.1981, and extended optimization is much slower. BigVGAN NLL increases from 200 to 500 steps while both WER and quality worsen. [Source/code diagnosis](/workspace/improvement/19_source_audit.md); [complete historical results](/workspace/improvement/16_all_results.md). The current engineering/restoration pilot may identify a useful control or repair, but its results must be reported before drawing any new conclusion.

Official ARR reviewer guidance, updated for October 2026, says that SOTA is neither necessary nor sufficient, welcomes sound negative findings and values efficiency/generalizability contributions; simplicity is not itself a weakness. It also asks for a clear knowledge gap, appropriate comparisons, reproducibility and statistical support. This supports pursuing an interpretable, useful result without manufacturing complexity or claiming that a higher headline WER is a paper. [ARR reviewer guidelines](https://aclrollingreview.org/reviewerguidelines).

For NLP fit, the principal result should concern speech-to-language processing: lexical error behavior, human recovery of original words, and cross-model generalization. GPU plumbing alone is a weaker fit. ARR area-chair guidance explicitly requires work on efficiency/security to contribute to human-language processing and demonstrate utility in natural-language evaluation. [ARR area-chair guidelines](https://aclrollingreview.org/acguidelines).

## Why the current repairs are useful but insufficient alone

Decoder graph retention removes redundant work while preserving the same mathematical objective. It supplies an implementation control and measured memory/time tradeoff. It does not explain when a lexical attack stays intelligible or generalizes. Fixed STFT blending is a standard signal intervention; it may diagnose or repair a particular distortion, but can also erase the magnitude evidence Whisper uses. A successful 48-point operating point would be valuable evidence, not by itself an established broadly useful method.

The issue is the scope of the current evidence and inherited mechanisms, not that simple methods are unpublishable. A large, reproducible efficiency improvement with a new generalizable finding could support an engineering contribution; a rigorous negative result could support analysis. Neither should be relabeled an original attack algorithm without evidence. More models, more steps, more data, EOT, generic MOS penalties, an extra spectrogram term, a standard generator, or their assembly are not automatic methodological novelty.

## Direction 1: lexical-coverage search that stops wasting distortion and compute

**Hypothesis, not a result:** after some reference words are already unstable, unbounded summed NLL spends disproportionate effort making those words even less probable. A search that spreads a bounded adversarial margin across still-correct lexical groups, while preserving a clean-reference speech constraint, can obtain the same decoded errors with less distortion and fewer expensive passes.

A concrete candidate to register after the current pilot is a **word-grouped saturating objective with sparse decoded feedback**. Group the source reference's subword tokens into words. Aggregate a declared correct-versus-alternative logit margin per word and cap its reward at a fixed margin. Every fixed number of updates, decode the permitted source and align its hypothesis to the reference; suspend already-corrupted word groups rather than rewarding longer insertion strings. Use a fixed clean-reconstruction spectral/envelope anchor and a declared total wall budget. Charge every decoded refresh, content calculation, and model pass. No target decode or MOS-gradient optimization is allowed.

This design is distinct from restarting the failed N-best R1: it does not require an erroneous N-best set to create a nonzero initial risk gradient. It is also more specific than globally clipping CE or early stopping. The mechanism claim would concern **distribution of lexical changes over the utterance per unit distortion and compute**, measured through loss concentration, S/D/I, decoded word coverage, and human original-word recovery. Its cap, lexical grouping and schedule remain design choices until frozen in a new protocol; no setting is claimed optimal here.

What might be new is a validated relationship between concentrated likelihood optimization, actual lexical-coverage saturation and speech distortion, together with a budgeted rule exploiting that relationship across ASR families. The individual pieces are known. If ordinary capped CE, a fixed shorter trajectory, or conventional early stopping explains the gains, downgrade the novelty claim to that simpler finding. If human word recovery falls, then high machine WER is not the desired adversarial preservation, regardless of MOS.

Necessary controls are original sum NLL; token-normalized NLL; a global bounded margin/capped CE; plain source-only decoded early stopping; word grouping without decoded masking; decoded masking without grouping; and the full method. Compare the same decoder and attack access, one matched wall budget, and one matched source-backward budget. Add or remove the spectral/envelope anchor separately. An independent phonetic/content model, if introduced, becomes a declared generation source and must be compared against a cheaper acoustic anchor; an ASR used for content preservation cannot remain an unseen target.

**Proposed research target:** at least a twofold reduction in complete generation time against a relevant quality-admissible comparator while meeting the unchanged joint gate, or a clear quality improvement at the same measured time and WER. This is a material-efficiency target proposed for this research, not a user latency limit. The present single50 loop is 19.61 s/recording, but full preprocessing and decoded-feedback costs must be measured before converting the target to seconds. A twofold algorithmic improvement is more consequential than the currently hypothesized small duplicate-render saving. Stop this method claim if no 48-point joint pass emerges within the registered budget or if decoded refreshes consume the hoped-for savings.

## Direction 2: learn the quality–cost frontier, conditionally on a successful teacher

**Conditional hypothesis:** an input-conditioned policy that predicts a small sequence of useful feature updates and a stopping decision can preserve a teacher's admissible lexical-confusion behavior over a range of compute budgets. Training solely on the final, heavily optimized delta may instead copy unnecessary distortion; learning only adversarial loss may reproduce the earlier near-zero generator collapse.

A potential implementation learns from **quality-qualified intermediate teacher trajectories**, with budget as an input, rather than copying only final deltas. It emits either a stop or a bounded residual update; zero/few optional source-gradient corrections give an explicit deployment curve. Its possible contribution is retained human-content/transfer performance over a measurable lifetime-cost frontier and a validated reason that trajectory/budget supervision helps. It is not “the first fast latent generator,” “the first learned attack,” or “distillation is new.” Generic learned optimizers and generator conditioning are established ideas, so this remains a high-collision direction requiring more focused prior-work review before a novelty claim.

Do not launch it merely because the ten-hour manifest exists. First demonstrate a joint-passing teacher on 48 development points, then establish useful targets on a disjoint training subset. Retain teacher failures and their cost; do not silently curate only easy speakers. Compare final-delta distillation, trajectory/budget supervision, the same-size adversarial-from-scratch policy, a fixed universal latent perturbation, and a fixed step-count policy. Match parameter count, training data and total training compute. Report zero/one/few-correction-step outputs separately and use multiple seeds for the learned comparison.

**Proposed research target:** at least tenfold measured new-audio generation speedup including required feature encoding/decoding at the unchanged gate, with lifetime cost reported at 1,000 and 10,000 recordings. Those are illustrative amortization workloads, not user-specified traffic. A small student forward alone does not qualify. Stop before expensive training if the teacher cannot pass, a bounded supervised feasibility run cannot recover teacher edits on disjoint development, or the forecasted teacher/training budget would overwhelm the intended benefit.

## Evidence package needed for an NLP/speech paper

1. **Freeze what is being claimed.** WER remains strictly >75%, DNSMOS OVRL ≥3, NISQA ≥3.75 and UTMOS ≥3.5 on complete cohorts. Give tails/failures as well as means. Distinguish source attacks, unseen-ASR transfer, preprocessing persistence, human content, and computational practicality. Report S/D/I, output lengths, empty outputs and token limits; supplementary lexical/semantic measures cannot replace the fixed WER endpoint.
2. **Generalize beyond the repeatedly studied 48.** Use 48 for each performance pilot. Only after source qualification and a feasible budget, freeze one candidate before the exposed 76-point validation, then untouched confirmation/final evaluation under their existing contracts. The 76 points share speakers and are development, not a fresh final test. The 48-point listening browser is exploratory. Preserve reserved outcomes until the registered gate permits their use.
3. **Add an independent speech domain.** LibriSpeech is read English audiobooks, not conversational/robust multilingual speech. Its [official corpus record](https://www.openslr.org/12) documents that domain. A second corpus such as TED-LIUM gives a separate speech domain; the [authors' corpus paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04699) documents it. Pin splits, licenses, segmentation and clean recognizer accuracy before acquisition/evaluation; a queried OpenSLR51 landing page currently returned unavailable, so availability is not assumed. A multilingual claim additionally needs a separately registered language/cohort and appropriate word/character normalization, not a reinterpretation of Chinese CER as English WER.
4. **Use architecture-diverse unseen models and actual defenses.** The four reserved recognizers remain untouched in development. Generation sources stay sources even if called through a content loss. Compare raw and transformed clean/reconstruction/candidate/control, including withheld transform parameters, and assess each defense separately. Resampling WER is not bypass of the complete WaveGuard detector. Do not claim defenses without executable checkpoints/recipes; missing learned defenses remain an explicit limitation.
5. **Measure original-word preservation in humans.** Blind and randomize conditions. Collect transcription without showing reference/ASR text, then score human WER against the original words; collect naturalness separately. Include original, reconstruction, quality control, strong baseline and fixed candidate. Control repeated-sentence exposure to prevent priming. Use speaker/listener-aware intervals and a sample size chosen for a predeclared precision or noninferiority question; choose its numerical margin prospectively rather than after outcomes. Unvalidated automatic “semantic similarity” is not a substitute. Match prior works' human endpoints only when they actually test the same construct.
6. **Make linguistic analysis explain the mechanism.** Predeclare tests for word/token frequency, phonetic class, duration, speech rate and substitution/deletion/insertion patterns; validate alignments on a subset. Check whether gains are broad lexical confusion or a few hallucinating examples. Avoid posthoc subgroup claims from 48 recordings. Analyze whether the intervention preserves human words yet changes machine decoding, rather than changing the spoken content.
7. **Provide reproducible compute-fair baselines.** Include the independent original FV implementation, strongest quality-admissible waveform baseline, SAGO/momentum adaptation, an appropriate quality-oriented method, and executable nearest learned/latent comparison if claiming amortization. State adaptation gaps. Hold model access, hardware, precision, clip lengths, preprocessing and stopping rules fixed for causal comparisons. Report both equal-wall and equal-gradient-call curves, peak allocated/reserved memory and per-recording median/p95 seconds/audio-second. Model size alone is not compute fairness.
8. **Quantify uncertainty and lifetime cost.** Use paired speaker-cluster bootstrap intervals for principal WER/MOS/time differences, and independent seeds for learned methods. Separate teacher preprocessing/target creation, training, loading/warmup, generation, repair and evaluation; retain failed runs. CUDA-event elapsed time is not kernel-active time or energy. Release fixed manifests, exact checkpoints/revisions, audio hashes and execution receipts within artifact terms. ARR's [Responsible NLP checklist](https://aclrollingreview.org/responsibleNLPresearch/) explicitly asks for computational resources, reproducibility, data provenance and human-study details.

## Cost discipline and next decision

Use measured relevant throughput before every long launch. Existing single50 loop throughput forecasts about **15.7 minutes per 48 pilot recordings**, **5.45 hours per 1,000 similarly distributed recordings**, or **26.14 GPU-allocation hours per ten audio hours** under duration-linear extrapolation. Those forecasts exclude loading, feature preprocessing and evaluation. Whisper's fixed context means clip-count and audio-duration forecasts have different assumptions; benchmark the actual proposed training-length distribution. They must not be replaced with the 45h51m cost of the old four-trajectory queue.

For Direction 1, first benchmark added decoded feedback on short/median/long exposed recordings as engineering checks, then forecast the fixed 48-point pilot with all conditions included. Register a ceiling from those measured costs; a provisional two-hour ceiling is a budget proposal, not permission to skip forecasting or quietly extend a failed job. Preserve the running pilot's original ceiling. For Direction 2, measure teacher and training throughput before choosing one versus ten hours of data. Lifetime cost is `teacher creation + training + N × measured generation`; compare that with `N × matched per-recording optimization` and report the break-even workload, including failure rates.

The next step after the current pilot is a decision based on its joint quality/runtime result. Keep its graph-reuse/restoration conditions as controls. Pursue one publication hypothesis at a time, and retain negative evidence if it fails. A manuscript becomes justified by a supported, appropriately scoped contribution and complete evidence—not by promising acceptance or renaming an engineering repair.

## Measured feasibility warning for Direction 1

The completed pilot supplies an important cost correction: native beam5 decoding on raw50 outputs averaged **4.298 s/recording**, compared with **16.774 s** for the entire 50-update retained-graph loop. Ten such refreshes conditionally add 43.0 s and would make this method substantially slower. This is not a forecast for greedy or intermediate-state decoding, whose costs remain unmeasured. The next engineering check must compare a no-feedback word-grouped cap with infrequent/cheaper feedback and account for every decode. Reject schedules whose predicted feedback cost consumes their search savings. [Measured forecast calibration](execution_tradeoff/forecast_calibration.json). No new GPU run was launched for this proposal.
